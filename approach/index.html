Principles

What we hold to.

  • We say "wait" more than "go."

    Most launches don't deserve your time. Wait and Ignore are common verdicts in Signals, and we'll tell you when a popular tool isn't right for you.

    In practice: we only recommend a tool once it passes the six questions below.

  • Your accounts, your data.

    We set tools up inside accounts you own, with the least access they need.

    In practice: you hold the logins, and you can remove our access at any time.

  • Plain English.

    If we can't explain why a launch matters to your business in two sentences, it doesn't go out.

    In practice: every tool we set up comes with a one-page guide to how you use it.

Six questions before anything gets a go.

A new tool has to pass all six before we recommend setting it up. Plenty of launches fail one. That's usually a wait.

Where we can, we try a tool ourselves before we recommend it.

  1. Does it do a job your team does every week?

    If we can't name the job and the person who does it, the answer is no.

  2. Can you use it now?

    It has to be available to your account, on the plan you have or one you'd actually pay for.

  3. Does it fit the tools you already use?

    Email, calendar, docs, CRM. A tool that can't connect to them rarely becomes part of the day.

  4. Is it safe for your data?

    Who can see what, where the data goes, and whether it needs to touch client data at all.

  5. Is it worth the cost and the learning time?

    The price per seat plus the hours to learn it, weighed against the time it gives back.

  6. Can you leave?

    You should be able to export your work and switch it off without losing anything.

Security basics

The rules for every setup.

Whatever the tool, these four apply from the first day.

  1. Least access.

    Every tool gets the smallest set of permissions it needs to do its job. If it only needs your calendar, it doesn't get your inbox.

  2. You own the accounts.

    Tools are set up in accounts your business owns and pays for. We work with the access you give us, and you can remove it at any time.

  3. A small, supervised test first.

    Every setup starts with 1 to 3 people before the whole team uses it. We fix what the test turns up, then roll it out.

  4. Nothing sends without your rules.

    You decide what an assistant must never send without a person approving it. During the test, a person approves every outgoing message.

Client and patient data. We don't connect a tool to protected health information unless the tool supports a business associate agreement (BAA) and you have one in place.
