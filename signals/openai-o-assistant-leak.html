OpenAI’s Rumored “o” Assistant: What Business Owners Need to Know Before Tuesday

An unconfirmed leak points to a possible always-on assistant. Here is what is known before Tuesday, and what your business can prepare now.

Unconfirmed report. Checked September 27, 2026 at 1:12 a.m. Pacific. This is not an OpenAI product announcement.

There is a new AI rumor worth watching if your business spends too much time sorting messages, arranging appointments and chasing replies. Reports suggest OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, may be preparing an assistant called “o.” We do not yet know what it does, who could use it or when it might become available.

Here is what the evidence means for a business owner, and what you can prepare without betting on a rumor.

What leaked

Pasquale Pillitteri reports that a user shared a ChatGPT Pro upgrade screenshot on September 25 containing the words “o, your always-on assistant.” The report also describes a configuration screenshot attributed to TestingCatalog, showing an “o” display name and an email-related identifier.

Kingy AI stresses that the screenshots have not been independently reproduced against OpenAI’s systems. An email-related identifier does not prove an assistant can read or send mail. OrcaRouter likewise separates staged wording from demonstrated behavior. These stories largely trace back to the same artifacts; repeating them does not turn speculation into confirmation.

The useful clue is the proposed direction: an assistant that could stay involved in work over time. The missing piece is a reliable description of the actual product.

What “always-on” could mean in practice

Think of an assistant with a continuing responsibility, rather than a chat you have to restart each time. Whether “o” would offer that experience is unknown. These three examples describe possible business workflows, not verified features of the rumored product.

Inbox: A roofing company could ask an assistant to collect new quote inquiries each morning, identify missing addresses and prepare replies for an employee to review. The benefit would be a clear queue of next steps. It should not promise a price or service date on its own.

Scheduling: A property manager could have an assistant gather availability for a maintenance visit and suggest a time that fits the tenant and contractor. A coordinator would confirm access and approve the booking. Success means fewer rounds of messages, with a person still responsible for the appointment.

Follow-ups: A small consulting firm could use an assistant to flag proposals that have gone unanswered and draft a reminder based on the last conversation. Someone would check the message before it goes out. The goal would be consistent follow-through, without pestering people who declined.

The right test is whether a workflow finishes accurately and saves your team time. A catchy product label cannot answer that.

Does this deserve comparisons to ChatGPT’s launch?

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT on November 30, 2022. Predicting another moment of that scale would be speculation. The available evidence is too limited to support that comparison.

Our view is that the idea attracts interest because owners want help carrying work forward, not just writing an answer. If a future assistant can do that reliably, it could change a team’s daily routine. The leak alone cannot tell us whether this one will.

What is confirmed, and what remains rumor

OpenAI’s official DevDay page confirms Tuesday, September 29 in San Francisco, with the opening keynote at 10 a.m. Pacific and a public livestream. It does not promise an “o” announcement.

At our check, no official “o” announcement was found in OpenAI’s news, ChatGPT release notes or public pricing page. The name, supported apps, permissions, price, eligible plans and release date remain unconfirmed. Even an onstage announcement would not necessarily mean everyone could use it that day.

What to do now

Pick one recurring job that slows your team down. Write down its steps, who owns it, what information it needs and what requires approval. Collect a few ordinary examples with sensitive details removed. Decide what a good result looks like: fewer missed inquiries, faster draft replies or less scheduling back-and-forth.

Then decide what an assistant may access and what it may change. Start with a small trial and review the results. There is no need to buy a subscription because of a leaked screenshot. This preparation will help you evaluate whatever is announced, and choose an available tool if the rumor does not become a product.
