OpenAI's leaked "o" assistant: what business owners should know before Tuesday

A ChatGPT Pro screen briefly listed "o, your always-on assistant." Here is what leaked, what is confirmed, and what to do before OpenAI's DevDay keynote.

By Hyrum HurstPosted Sep 27, 2026, 1:30 AM MSTUpdated Sep 27, 2026

Status: unconfirmed. OpenAI has not announced a product called "o." We will update this page after the DevDay keynote on Tuesday, September 29 at 10:00 AM Pacific.

The short version

  • On September 25, a ChatGPT user posted a screenshot of the Pro upgrade screen. One line read "o, your always-on assistant."
  • The next day, TestingCatalog reported app configuration with "o" as a display name and "-o" as an email suffix.
  • OpenAI's DevDay is Tuesday. A launch there is possible, not promised.
  • If it ships as described, it is an assistant that keeps working after you close the chat. For a small business, that points at the inbox, the calendar and the follow-ups nobody gets to.

What actually leaked

Two things, both posted by users and neither verified by OpenAI.

The first is a ChatGPT Pro upgrade screen that listed "o" among the plan's perks, described as an always-on assistant. The second is configuration text inside the app: a display name of "o" and an email suffix of "-o."

That is the entire evidence base. There is no feature list, no price and no release date. The Pro plan's price is the plan's price. It tells you nothing about whether "o" would cost extra.

What "always-on" would mean in practice

Today's ChatGPT waits for you. You open it, ask, and close it. An always-on assistant would keep working in the background. These are the three places a business would feel that first:

  1. The inbox. Sorting what needs you, drafting replies in your voice, and chasing threads that went quiet. The "-o" email suffix hints at email, though it could mean an address or an alias rather than access to your inbox.
  2. The calendar. Finding times, confirming appointments and handling reschedules without the back-and-forth.
  3. Follow-ups. The quotes that never got a second touch, and the leads that went cold because everyone was busy.

These are inferences from the name and the configuration, not confirmed features.

Why people are comparing it to November 2022

When ChatGPT launched on November 30, 2022, most businesses spent the next year catching up. People in AI circles are calling "o" a similar moment because it would move AI from a tool you visit to a worker that runs. That comparison is hype until OpenAI ships something. We pass it along so you know what you will hear this week, not because we can confirm it.

Who this is for, and who can skip it

Worth your attention if your week is eaten by email, scheduling or follow-ups, and you already pay for ChatGPT Plus, Pro or Business. Safe to skip for now if your team does not use ChatGPT at all, or if client data rules mean a new assistant would need a long approval process anyway.

What to do before Tuesday

Verdict: Prepare. You do not need to buy anything yet. You can get ready so you can move on day one if it ships:

  • Clean up the shared inbox. Archive dead threads and set up labels for sales, support and billing. An assistant works better on an organized inbox.
  • Write down your three most repetitive email types. Booking confirmations, quote follow-ups and "just checking in." Those are the first jobs to hand off.
  • Check who owns your accounts. Know which Google or Microsoft account holds the company email and calendar, and who can grant access.
  • Decide your rules. What should an assistant never send without a person approving it? Write that down now, while it is calm.
