OpenAI's leaked "o" assistant after DevDay: what "dots" means for business owners

A ChatGPT Pro upgrade screen reportedly listed "o, your always-on assistant." At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI announced "dots," always-on agents in ChatGPT. It did not announce anything called "o." Here is what was announced, who can use it now, and what to do.

Status, Oct 1: OpenAI announced "dots," not "o." On September 29, OpenAI announced dots, always-on agents in ChatGPT. None of the OpenAI pages we checked use the name "o." Dots matches the leak closely, but OpenAI has not said they are the same product.

Updated Oct 1: what DevDay announced

Announced on Sept 29

  • OpenAI announced dots, which it calls always-on agents in ChatGPT. TechCrunch reports they run on GPT-6 Astra.
  • A dot keeps working between chats. It can run scheduled reminders and recurring checks, such as a morning review of your calendar.
  • When you are not working with it, a dot researches in the background with read-only tools. Those tools cannot send messages, change app content or control a browser.
  • You can set limits. Custom Rules let you allow an action, require approval or block it. Before a dot sends an email or changes files, a separate check called Auto-review compares the step with your instructions and rules. Purchases with saved cards need your approval. Password changes and money transfers go back to you.
  • You can message or call your dot in ChatGPT on desktop, web and mobile, and message it in Slack and Teams.

Who can use it now

The rollout started Sept 29 and is gradual. OpenAI's Help Center lists who gets it and says access may take several days to reach an account.

  • Pro: rolling out in eligible markets, except the EEA, Switzerland and the UK.
  • Business Premium: rolling out in all supported ChatGPT regions.
  • Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare: a beta, off by default until a workspace admin turns it on (DevDay recap).
  • Free, Go and Plus: not included. Among individual plans, OpenAI's pricing page lists dots only under Pro.
  • Cost: your first dot is included in Pro or Business Premium at no extra cost. Chatting with it does not count toward usage limits. Tasks it starts in Codex or ChatGPT Work do.
  • Limits at launch: a dot can use a personal email account you connect, but it cannot have its own email address or call you. Texting is a limited beta for some US Pro users.

Coming later, with no date

  • Wider access. OpenAI says it plans to bring dots "to more users soon" and names no plans.
  • Usage terms after the first month. The release notes say dots usage will not count toward plan allowances for the next month, and that OpenAI will share usage terms for each plan after that. The launch post describes the first month differently, as extended limits.
  • More than one dot. Adding more dots, or scaling a dot's speed or monthly work, is described as coming in the future.
  • Broader texting. OpenAI's launch post calls texting "coming soon."

Not announced

Anything called "o." The name is not in OpenAI's dots post, DevDay recap, dots safety post, Help Center article, release notes or Pro tiers article.

What changes for a working business

  • On Free, Go or Plus: nothing for now. Dots is not on your plan.
  • On Pro (outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK) or Business Premium: your dot may already be there. If not, OpenAI says access may take several days. Write your Custom Rules before you connect email.
  • On Enterprise: nothing happens until an admin turns the beta on. Decide who tests it first.
  • For everyone: the prep list below still applies, and written approval rules map straight onto Custom Rules.

The verdict stays Prepare. Dots is limited to Pro, Business Premium and an Enterprise beta, and the usage terms after the first month are not public yet.

What actually leaked

Two screenshots, both posted on X and neither confirmed by OpenAI.

The first, posted by Jake Boggs on September 25, is a "Get ChatGPT Pro" upgrade screen. Under "Everything in Plus, and:" it lists "o, your always-on assistant."

The second, posted by TestingCatalog the next day, shows ChatGPT's internal configuration with a display name of "o" and an email suffix of "-o." TestingCatalog says the line appeared only briefly on the upgrade screen, for some users.

That was the entire evidence base before DevDay. There was no feature list, no release date and no separate price. On the leaked screen, "o" was a Pro perk on top of Plus. Whether Plus, Business or Enterprise would get it was unknown, and TestingCatalog said the assistant's tasks, permissions, scheduling, memory and availability remained unknown. DevDay answered most of those questions for dots.

Is dots the "o" from the leak?

Probably, but OpenAI has not said so.

  • What matches. The leak showed an always-on assistant listed as a Pro perk. OpenAI's pricing page now lists "Dot, your always-on agent" under Pro.
  • What does not. The leak pointed to an "-o" email suffix. At launch, a dot cannot have its own email address, and its default handle is @yourname-dot.
  • What is still open. The night before the keynote, TestingCatalog reported that the agents would be called "Dots" and asked whether "o" was "something else on top of it." Its Sept 30 launch article does not mention "o."

Our read: the same product under its launch name. That is our inference, not OpenAI's statement.

What "always-on" means in practice

Before DevDay, all we had was the word "assistant" and an email suffix. ChatGPT Work could already run scheduled tasks that repeat or watch for changes, and since August 25, Plus and Pro users can have a task respond when a new Gmail message arrives. OpenAI says dots go further: they keep making progress between conversations and look for ways to help in the background. These are the three places a business would feel it first:

  1. The inbox. A dot can use a personal email account you connect, and Auto-review checks the recipient and the message before it sends. TestingCatalog read the leaked "-o" suffix to mean "o" would handle email. As Kingy AI pointed out, a suffix alone could also mean an address of its own. At launch, a dot cannot have one.
  2. The calendar. A dot can review your calendar each morning and tell you what is coming up. That example is OpenAI's own.
  3. Follow-ups. Scheduled reminders and recurring checks are the building blocks. Using them to chase quotes and cold leads is our reading.

Who this is for, and who can skip it

Prepare if your week is eaten by email, scheduling or follow-ups and your team already uses ChatGPT. If you already pay for Pro or a Business Premium seat, write your rules this week and give your dot one small recurring job when it arrives. Skip it for now if your team does not use ChatGPT at all, if you are on Pro in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK, or if client data rules mean a new agent would need a long approval process anyway.

What to do now

No need to buy anything yet. These steps pay off whether dots is on your plan today or not:

  • Clean up the shared inbox. Archive dead threads and set up labels for sales, support and billing. An agent works better on an organized inbox.
  • Write down your three most repetitive email types. Booking confirmations, quote follow-ups and "just checking in." Those are the first jobs to hand off.
  • Check who owns your accounts. Know which Google or Microsoft account holds the company email and calendar, and who can grant access.
  • Write your rules. What should an agent never send without a person approving it? Dots' Custom Rules let you allow an action, require approval or block it, so a written list carries straight over.
  • Start with one recurring check. If your dot has arrived, give it one job that only reads, such as a morning review of your calendar. If it has not, ChatGPT Work can already run a scheduled task when a new Gmail message arrives on Plus and Pro, and actions that need approval wait for you.
