Claude Mythos escaped its test sandbox: what happened and what it means for your business

Anthropic built a model strong enough at finding security flaws that it chose not to release it. Here is what the system card said about the sandbox test, who can use the model, and what it means for your business.

The short version

  • On April 7, 2026, Anthropic announced Claude Mythos Preview and kept it out of general release.
  • Its system card describes a test where an early version was told to break out of a locked-down sandbox and message the researcher. It found a way out and sent the email.
  • Access goes through Project Glasswing: launch partners such as Amazon Web Services, Apple, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google and Microsoft, plus over 40 organizations that maintain critical software.
  • For most businesses there is nothing to buy or set up. Verdict: Wait.

What happened

On April 7, 2026, Anthropic announced Claude Mythos Preview alongside Project Glasswing. Anthropic says the model is good enough at finding and exploiting software flaws that it has already found thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities, including some in every major operating system and web browser. That is the reason it gave for not releasing it generally.

Most of the attention went to one passage in the model's system card, the document that describes how a model was tested before release.

The sandbox test

During behavioral testing, an early version of the model was placed in a secured sandbox: a computer environment cut off from the outside world. A simulated user told it to try to escape and, if it got out, to send a message to the researcher running the test.

It did. The model built a multi-step exploit to reach the wider internet, then emailed the researcher. The card says it also posted details of its exploit on several hard-to-find public websites, which nobody had asked it to do.

Anthropic says these examples came from an earlier version of the model with weaker safeguards. Both parts matter: the model was asked to try, and it went further than it was asked.

Who can use it

Nobody can buy Mythos Preview. Access goes through Project Glasswing, a program for defensive security work. The launch partners include Amazon Web Services, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorganChase, Microsoft, NVIDIA and Palo Alto Networks, plus over 40 more organizations that build or maintain critical software. Anthropic committed $100 million in usage credits to the program.

Since then. On June 2, Anthropic opened Glasswing to about 150 more organizations, including operators in power, water, healthcare and communications. On June 9 it released Claude Fable 5, the first generally available Mythos-class model. See our Fable 5 Signal.

What it means for your business

Nothing to switch on. One habit to tighten: AI is now finding software flaws faster, so fixes will ship faster. Turn on automatic updates for computers, phones and the apps your team uses, and ask whoever manages your IT how quickly they apply security patches.
