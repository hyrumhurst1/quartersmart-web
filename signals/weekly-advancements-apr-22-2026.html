The week of April 21, 2026: longer context windows, agents showing up in public-sector buying, and data residency becoming the first question.

The short version

  • Frontier models now take in about a million tokens of context, but most people still paste in one paragraph at a time.
  • Agents are moving from novelty to procurement: public buyers now ask for audit logs, data handling and training, not just a demo.
  • Where the data lives is becoming the first question in any pilot that touches regulated records.

Every week something in AI shifts: a new model, a new policy, a new product tier. Most of it is noise. Here is what moved in the week of April 21, 2026, and what it means for a team deciding where AI fits.

1. Long context goes mainstream, and usability lags

The top models keep pushing how much they can read at once. OpenAI's GPT-5.4, released in March, takes in about a million tokens through its API. On paper that is a big deal: give a model a full contract, a policy manual or a year of meeting notes, and it can work across all of it.

In practice, most people still paste in one paragraph at a time. The capability is ahead of the habit, and that gap is where adoption is won or lost.

What it means for your team

A million-token window is wasted on someone who doesn't know they can paste the whole document at once. The most underrated AI habit right now is "here is the full context, now help me."

2. Agents move into procurement

US federal agencies have been able to buy ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude and Gemini through the General Services Administration since August 2025. The questions have moved on from "can it do the task" to "can it do the task with audit logs, careful handling of personal data, a clear answer on where the data lives, and training for the people using it."

That is a useful checklist for any business, not only public agencies. A tool shipped without training mostly ships frustration.

3. Where the data lives is the first question

In government, schools and regulated businesses, the first question is usually some version of: "Where does our data live, and who can see it?"

Vendors are answering it. OpenAI, for example, now lets eligible ChatGPT Enterprise, Edu and API customers choose the region where their data is stored. Private cloud and on-premises options are moving from premium extras toward a baseline expectation for anyone handling sensitive records.

What we were watching next

  • Agent evaluation. The field still lacks a good "does this work in a real job" test.
  • Procurement language. Contract terms for AI tools, including data location and audit rights.
  • Local-host model releases. Open-weight models for on-premises use keep improving.
