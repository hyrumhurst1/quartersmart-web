Anthropic's Project Glasswing: who can use Claude Mythos Preview, and why it is not public

Anthropic kept its strongest security model private and gave it to a group of software and security companies for defensive work. Here is who is in the program, how it works, and what it means for a business outside it.

What Glasswing is

Project Glasswing is how Anthropic handles a model it considers too capable at cyber attacks to sell openly. On April 7, 2026, it announced Claude Mythos Preview and, in the same breath, the program that decides who gets to use it.

The idea is simple. Anthropic says Mythos Preview has already found thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities, including some in every major operating system and web browser. If a model can do that, the people who maintain the software should have it first, so they can find and fix the flaws before attackers do.

Who is in it

The launch partners Anthropic named are Amazon Web Services, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorganChase, Microsoft, NVIDIA and Palo Alto Networks, along with a major open-source software foundation. Over 40 more organizations that build or maintain critical software infrastructure joined at launch.

On June 2, 2026, Anthropic expanded the program to about 150 more organizations in more than 15 countries, including operators in power, water, healthcare and communications.

How it works

Partners use the model for defensive security work on software they build or depend on. Anthropic committed $100 million in usage credits for Mythos Preview across the program, and $4 million in direct donations to open-source security organizations.

Anthropic has not made Mythos Preview available to buy. On June 9 it released Claude Fable 5, a Mythos-class model with safeguards for general use. Our Fable 5 Signal covers what changed.

What it means for your business

Nothing to adopt. The practical effect is indirect: when the companies behind your operating systems, browsers and cloud tools find flaws faster, security updates arrive faster. Make sure automatic updates are on for your computers, phones and the apps your team uses.
